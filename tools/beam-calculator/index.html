About section properties

Sections are laid end-to-end from x = 0. Total beam length is the sum of the segment lengths. Each segment may have its own material and geometry, giving a stepped or tapered (stepwise) beam.

  • κ is the shear correction factor: shear area As = κA. Typical values — rectangle 5/6, circle 0.9, thin tube 0.5, I-beam ≈ Aweb/A. Only used by Timoshenko theory.
  • G = E / (2(1+ν)).
  • c is the extreme-fibre distance used for σ = Mc/I. Set to 0 to skip the stress diagram.

Support types
  • Pinned / roller — v = 0, rotation free.
  • Fixed — v = 0 and θ = 0 (built-in / clamped).
  • Guided (slider) — θ = 0, vertical translation free.
  • Vertical spring — elastic support of stiffness Kv (force/length).
  • Rotational spring — elastic restraint Kθ (moment/radian).
  • Pin + rot. spring, Vert. + rot. spring — combinations for partially fixed ends.
  • Δ imposes a support settlement (positive = downward), θ0 an imposed rotation on a fixed/guided support.

Theory & Method

How this tool works

The beam is solved by the direct stiffness method with 2 degrees of freedom per node (transverse displacement v and rotation θ). Nodes are placed at every section change, support, point load and distributed-load boundary, and each interval is then subdivided into a fine mesh.

Euler–Bernoulli elements use the classical Hermitian cubic stiffness matrix (plane sections remain plane and normal — no shear deformation).

Timoshenko elements use the shear-flexible (interdependent interpolation) stiffness matrix, which is exact for the homogeneous solution and free of shear locking:

Φ = 12EI / (κGA L²)    k = EI/(L³(1+Φ)) · [[12, 6L, -12, 6L], [6L, (4+Φ)L², -6L, (2−Φ)L²], [-12, -6L, 12, -6L], [6L, (2−Φ)L², -6L, (4+Φ)L²]]

Distributed loads are applied as consistent nodal load vectors. Shear and bending moment diagrams are then recovered by statics — integrating the applied loads and the computed reactions from the left-hand end — so the diagrams satisfy equilibrium exactly and are independent of the element interpolation. Deflections and rotations come from the nodal solution.

Sign conventions: v positive upward, θ positive counter-clockwise, sagging bending moment positive, shear positive when the left-hand free body is pushed upward. Applied loads are entered positive downward and applied moments positive counter-clockwise.

The Timoshenko rotation θ is the rotation of the cross-section; the slope of the neutral axis is dv/dx = θ + γ, where γ = V/(κGA) is the shear strain.

Assumptions & limitations
  • Linear-elastic, small-displacement analysis of a straight, planar beam.
  • Bending about one principal axis only; no axial force, torsion or P-Δ effects.
  • Section properties are constant within each segment (a taper is modelled as a series of steps).
  • Bending stress σ = Mc/I ignores stress concentrations and shear stress.
  • Use the Verify button to check the solver against closed-form solutions.
