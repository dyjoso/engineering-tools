User guide (click to expand)

What it does. Gives the skin stress around an elliptical region whose thickness differs from the surrounding skin: a doubler (thicker, attracts load — the skin at its ends is critical), a blend-out (thinner, sheds load — the skin beside it and the thinned region itself are critical) or an open hole (tr = 0).

Inputs. L is the region length along σx0, W its width along σy0. For a doubler, tr is skin + doubler; for a blend, the remaining thickness. Ed is used only for a doubler (a blend uses Es). Tension is positive.

Rectangular doubler. Enter its L × W: it is represented by the ellipse with semi-axes L/2, W/2. The centreline end stress is then over-predicted by about 3–11% (conservative). Corners carry a local peak not modelled here.

Results. Stresses are in the skin at the region ends (on the σx0 axis), sides (on the σy0 axis) and within the region. At these points σx and σy are principal stresses by symmetry. The peak is the largest of them. Save / Open keep the inputs as a .json file.

Method (Appendix A summary)

The region is an elliptical inhomogeneity in an infinite skin. With equal Poisson’s ratios, a region of thickness tr behaves in-plane as skin of thickness ts with its modulus scaled by the membrane stiffness ratio

Γ = (Ests + Edtd) / (Ests)  (doubler),    Γ = tr / ts  (blend),    Γ = 0  (hole).

Equivalent inclusion (Eshelby 1957): the strain inside the ellipse is uniform,

εin = [ I + (Γ − 1) S ]−1 ε0,    Eε0 = { σx0 − νσy0 , σy0 − νσx0 }

with the plane-stress Eshelby tensor for semi-axes a = L/2 (along x) and b = W/2 (with a = 1, b = r):

S1111 = r[(2+r)(1+ν) + (1+r)(1−ν)] / 2(1+r)²,   S1122 = r[r(1+ν) − (1+r)(1−ν)] / 2(1+r)²
S2211 = [(1+ν) − (1+r)(1−ν)] / 2(1+r)²,   S2222 = [(1+2r)(1+ν) + (1+r)(1−ν)] / 2(1+r)²

The skin material in the region carries σin = E/(1−ν²)[1 ν; ν 1] εin; the running load there is Γ ts σin. At the boundary, the normal running load and the tangential strain are continuous, giving the skin just outside:

Ends: σx,end = Γ σinxx,   σy,end = Eεinyy + νΓ σinxx     Sides: σy,side = Γ σinyy,   σx,side = Eεinxx + νΓ σinyy

The doubler stress is (Ed/Es) σin. For a circular hole the solution returns Kirsch (σx,side = 3σx0, σy,end = −σx0). References: Eshelby, Proc. Roy. Soc. A 241 (1957); Mura, Micromechanics of Defects in Solids (1987); Hardiman, QJMAM 7 (1954).

5. Notes and limitations

  1. Exact for an elliptical region with semi-axes L/2 and W/2 in an infinite, uniformly loaded skin (Eshelby 1957; identical to Baker/Rose Eqs. 8.1a–8.3). A far-field membrane solution: treat it as the input to local checks, not a replacement for them.
  2. Rectangular doubler: the end stress on the centreline is over-predicted by about 3–11% (conservative). Corners carry a local peak not modelled here — radius or taper them, or check them separately.
  3. Finite panels: within about 3–5 region widths of an edge, frame, stringer or cut-out the true stress is higher (typically 1–3%). Model the actual panel there.
  4. Assumes linear elastic membrane behaviour, a fully effective doubler, equal Poisson’s ratios and remote normal stresses aligned with the region axes (no remote shear).
  5. Not included: fastener shear lag and first-row bearing / bypass (check the first row with the attracted running load KfrontN0), secondary bending of one-sided doublers or blends, and the blend edge taper (full depth over the whole ellipse is conservative).
