07 Assumptions & Notes

Method & assumptions

  1. Module 1 — Pressure. The cabin-pressure differential is the cabin pressure minus the ambient pressure at the aircraft's max operating altitude. Pressures are interpolated linearly between the breakpoints of 14 CFR Part 43 Appendix E, Table I (standard-atmosphere altimeter test pressures), and converted in.Hg → psi with the standard factor 14.696 / 29.921 = 0.49116. Cabin altitude defaults to 8,000 ft (FAR Part 25 passenger-cabin limit). [14 CFR pt. 43 App. E; 727 TCDS A3WE]
  2. Suction allowance. An additional 0.5 psi (editable) is added to the operating differential for suction pressure, giving the max operational cabin pressure used for stress. The 727-200F example gives ΔP = 8.43 psi → 8.93 psi with suction — reproduced by this tool to the precision of Table I.
  3. Module 2 — Stresses. The four element stresses (σH, σL, σS, σF) are the simultaneous solution of: (a) circumferential equilibrium of one frame bay, (b) longitudinal equilibrium of one stringer bay, (c) frame/skin and (d) stringer/skin strain compatibility. Because all material is aluminium (constant E), compatibility is written directly in stress with Poisson coupling.
  4. Bending (RAPID). Fuselage 1g bending stress σB = ( Ckd·min(SRM·Ftu/1.5, SRM·Fty) − Cb·(P+Pa)R/2t ) / 2.5, then × kb × kp. Ckd = 0.88 (bending knockdown, fixed). SRM (default 1.0) is the net-section factor applied to Ftu and Fty, accounting for SRM repair fastener pitch (4D minimum, 5D depending on location). Cb = 0.85 (fixed) scales the longitudinal pressure stress subtracted from limit. Pa = 1.1 psi (fixed) is the pressure adder in the bending term. kb (bending factor, default 1.0) scales σB for the repair location along the fuselage length (1.0 = peak bending station). kp (payload factor, default 1.0) scales σB for less-than-maximum cargo/passenger loading (1.0 = maximum). d (default 0) is the offset of the frame centroid from the skin mid-plane, positive inboard: for the same radial displacement the frame hoop strain is R/(R−d) times the skin's, so the frame compatibility equation reads (1 − d/R)·σF = σH − νσL − νσB. The 1g σB enters the frame compatibility equation through its Poisson term νσB, and is added to the skin and stringer longitudinal stresses after the matrix solve. SEF (spectrum effects factor, default 1.3 — 1.3g by convention) lumps the flight spectrum into a single effective stress; it multiplies the bending component only, not pressure, giving the with-SEF stresses σL + SEF·σB and σS + SEF·σB. It is not applied in the compatibility solve or the static residual-strength cases. Method and defaults match the reference spreadsheet Pressure Stresses_Rigid Rev 02 V2.
  5. Thin-shell limit check. As AF, AS → 0 the skin recovers the bare-tube values σH → P R / t and σL → P R / 2t; the equilibrium-residual rows confirm the solve. Stiffeners and Poisson coupling redirect skin load into the frame/stringer, lowering skin hoop below P R / t.
  6. Link to Module 1. The stress pressure P is auto-filled with Module 1's max operational cabin pressure while the Link P box is checked. Uncheck it to enter a standalone pressure (e.g. a proof or ultimate case).
  7. Geometry convention. ℓ = frame pitch (circumferential bay length), s = stringer pitch (longitudinal bay length); AF, AS are the effective frame and stringer cross-section areas per bay. Skin effective widths are assumed already folded into AF / AS.
  8. Damage-tolerance use: results are operating elastic stresses for crack-growth / residual-strength work; they are not ultimate or yield margins. Combine with local stress concentrations and bending peaking separately.
  9. Residual strength (§05 table). Each component is decomposed into a pressure-only stress and a bending component, then two residual-strength load cases are evaluated per 14 CFR §25.571(b)(5): (1) pressure case, §25.571(b)(5)(ii) = pressure-only × the pressure residual factor (1.15 default, 1.10 selectable); (2) flight loads, §25.571(b)(5)(i) = pressure-only + 2.5× the bending component (limit flight loads). Bending shares: skin hoop = 0 (no bending); skin longitudinal & stringer = full σB; frame = the Poisson term νσB/(1 − d/R). The bending component is the 1g σB and does not carry SEF. The governing row (worst of the two cases across all four components) is highlighted.
